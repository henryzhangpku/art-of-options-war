# The Art of Options War · 期权兵法

**Sun Tzu's thirteen chapters, in his order and on his subjects, read from both ends of a defined-risk options ticket, the seller of the credit spread and the buyer of the option, and a fifth part on the general's own question: when to defend, and when to attack.**

*Second edition.* It absorbs two companion books, *The Options Underwriter* and *The Counted Trade*. The seller now reads the spread as an insurer reads a policy, and the buyer carries the Thirty-Six Stratagems, which stand whole in an appendix.

<img src="cover.jpg" alt="The Art of Options War" width="320">

Two and a half thousand years ago a general wrote thirteen short chapters on how to win without being destroyed. He was not interested in glory. He was interested in the arithmetic of survival: which battles to fight, which to refuse, how to be paid by an enemy's mistakes instead of your own courage, and why the state that wins every battle can still lose the war.

Options are the same problem in a different uniform, and the uniform has two sides. The seller is paid to hold a line. He does not need to know where the enemy will go, only where the enemy will not arrive in time. He wins most engagements and can be ruined by one. The buyer pays for the right to be right. He loses most engagements, each one small and paid for in advance, and lives on the rare one that pays many times over.

Every chapter reads the general twice. **The Seller** comes first: the credit spread, the hedge into a condor, the short leg cut and the wing kept. **The Buyer** follows: the debit as the whole risk, time as the enemy, volatility as the price, the catalyst as the only ally, and five sentences to say before paying — the edge, the catalyst, the maximum loss, the time stop and the breakeven win rate. The commentary reads both.

Numbered aphorisms with a commentary, in the manner of the Giles translation. No forecasts, no signals, no performance claims.

Part V, *Attack and Defence* 《攻守》, comes after *Spies*, because a trader cannot choose a stance until he has read what the fire costs. In it the buyer speaks first: the two banks of one river, the five grounds of attack, the tax most attackers pay, buying as insurance, and when to change sides.

The order is the argument: count before you fight, know what a campaign costs, win without needing to be right — or be right cheaply when you must be — make yourself unbeatable before you look for a chance, divide your force, read the ground, survive fire, value information above every other weapon, and only then choose whether today is a day to defend or to attack.

## Read one chapter a day

Then read them again in the general's order. Eighteen chapters, about two and a half weeks at one a day, and the second pass is where the order starts to do the work.

## A note on what this is

This book is education, not advice. It contains no recommendations for you personally, no signals and no promises. Options can lose money faster than stock. A defined-risk spread cannot lose more than its width, and a long option cannot lose more than its debit, but either can still be more than you can afford, and a run of them can still end an account. Nothing here is a performance claim; where the text names a number, the number is arithmetic you can check on paper.

## Corrections and translations

Corrections are welcome — open an issue or a pull request. If you find a place where the arithmetic in the commentary does not check out on paper, that is the most useful issue you can file.

The chapter files in `en/` and `zh/` are generated from the edition in the Henry Zhang Books app, so the two never disagree about the text. An accepted correction is made at that source and the chapters are regenerated; a pull request against a chapter file is the place to point at the fix, not the place it lands.

Translations into other languages are welcome as forks; tell me and I will link yours from here.
