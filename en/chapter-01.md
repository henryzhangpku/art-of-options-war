# 1. Counting in the Temple

*Part I: The Count*

1. The trade is a matter of vital importance to the account. It is the ground of survival or ruin. It cannot be entered on a whim.

2. Therefore, before the market opens, weigh the trade by five constants, and compare your side with the enemy's.

3. These are: the Sky, the Ground, the Commander, the Method, and the Cause.

4. The Sky is time. It is the option's expiry, the hours that remain, the decay that runs in one direction only. The Sky favours the seller, and the seller must never fight it.

5. The Ground is the terrain of prices: where the strikes stand, how wide the spread is, where the walls of dealer hedging rise and where the floors lie. The Ground decides how far the enemy must travel to reach you.

6. The Commander is the trader: his patience, his discipline, his willingness to take a small loss, his refusal to double a bet. All five constants are weighed, but the Commander is the one who fails.

7. The Method is the plan for money: how the day's risk is divided, when the hedge is sold, when the short leg is cut, when the wing is left to ride. A Method that exists only in the head does not exist.

8. The Cause is why you trade at all. If it is to be right, you will lose. If it is to be paid for standing behind a line, you may endure.

9. He who knows these five will prevail; he who does not will be surprised, and surprise is the seller's only enemy.

10. Therefore, before entering, ask: which side has the Sky? Whose Ground is easier to defend? Which Commander is calmer? Whose Method has a rule for being wrong? By these, the outcome is known.

11. All trading is based on positioning. When you are willing to sell, seem uninterested. When the market screams direction, remember that it is offering you a price for the fence, not a prophecy.

12. The seller who wins first counts in the temple. He knows his maximum loss before he knows his profit. He knows what he will do if the line is tested before it is tested. He knows the day can turn four times, and he has kept powder for each turn.

13. The seller who counts once wins once. The seller who counts every morning is still here in the afternoon, and in the year after.

## Commentary

The old text begins with counting because the temple is the only place a battle can be lost without cost. For the option seller the temple count is one sentence, said out loud before the order: *this is a five-wide spread sold for one, the maximum loss is four, and the loss at which I cut is one.* If the sentence cannot be said, the trade cannot be taken.

Of the five constants, only the Sky is reliably yours. Time decays in one direction, and a seller who does not fight it is paid every hour he is not wrong. The Ground can be chosen but not controlled. The Method can be written and then ignored. And the Commander is where every account dies: not from being wrong about direction, which is ordinary, but from refusing to be wrong at a small size.

The Cause is placed last because it is the quietest. A trader who needs to be right will hold a broken spread to prove a point. A trader who is paid to hold a line simply moves the line, or leaves the field. The rest of this book assumes the second man.
