# Appendix A: The Thirty-Six Stratagems for the Buyer

*三十六计，买方读本*

The Thirty-Six Stratagems are an anonymous Chinese collection, set down in its present form late in the imperial era, of tricks played on an enemy, in six sets of six. The first edition of this book's buyer had them at full length in a companion volume, *The Counted Trade*. In this edition they are quoted through the chapters where they fit, each cited by its number, and here they stand whole: the stratagem, its image, and a few lines for the buyer. Where a chapter reads a stratagem at length, the page says so.

The enemy most often tricked is yourself. Read them in their order, for the order is the argument: count before the battle, read the other side's price, pass the funnel, hold the maximum loss in chaos, test without fooling yourself, and know when to leave.

## Set I: Stratagems for Winning 《胜战计》

### 1. Cross the Sea Under Camouflage · 瞒天过海 (第1计)

*The emperor feared the sea, so a general built him a house upon the water, and he crossed believing he sat on land.*

1. The hope trade enters the account dressed as conviction and furnished like a plan. The camouflage is aimed at the passenger, and the passenger is the buyer.

2. The test of the floor is five sentences said aloud before the order: the edge, the catalyst, the maximum loss, the time stop, the breakeven win rate.

3. Say them where another can hear, or write them where the later self will read. A sentence that cannot be said is a wall painted on water.

4. Conviction belongs to the rules and the thesis, never to the size. A size that grows with feeling is the sea showing through the paint.

*In this book:* Chapter 1; the five sentences in full in Chapter 15.

### 2. Besiege Wei to Rescue Zhao · 围魏救赵 (第2计)

*When Wei besieged Handan, the relief rode not to Handan but to Daliang, the enemy's own capital, and the siege broke behind it.*

1. The price is Handan: loud, visible, and not where the trade is won. The mispricing is Daliang.

2. An edge is one sentence in three parts: I expect this mispricing, because of this measurable reason, and after costs this structure wins if this condition holds.

3. "I think it goes up" is a direction with a feeling attached. Feelings carry no mechanism.

4. After costs is not an afterthought. Commission, spread and decay are the road the army must march, and an edge that dies on the road was never an edge.

5. Where an outcome can only go two ways, the chain may still price one smooth curve. Besiege the curve, and the direction rescues itself.

*In this book:* Chapter 3.

### 3. Kill With a Borrowed Knife · 借刀杀人 (第3计)

*A knife is borrowed, not forged. The hand supplies the direction; another man's weight supplies the blow.*

1. The buyer's knife is dated: an event on a day, a mechanism that reprices the stock, and a chain that has not yet charged for either.

2. A catalyst without a date is a rumour. A date without a mechanism is a coin.

3. Keep one calendar of the next forty-five days. Company events, macro dates, legal and regulatory decisions, mechanical events like an index rebalance. What falls outside it is another trade, entered early.

4. If the date passes and the price has not moved, the knife was the wrong knife, and the trade is wrong even if the story is still good.

5. No dated mechanism, no catalyst. No catalyst, no trade. Time without an event is rent paid to nobody.

*In this book:* Chapter 3.

### 4. Wait at Leisure for the Exhausted Enemy · 以逸待劳 (第4计)

*The general does not chase. He holds the pass, keeps his men in the shade, and lets the enemy's march do half the work.*

1. For the buyer the march is time, and it tires the buyer, not the enemy. Every day held, the debit is eaten by an opponent who never sleeps.

2. Patience has a deadline: I leave on this date if the thesis has not confirmed, regardless of price.

3. Buy the time the thesis needs, or set the stop the time allows. Short time and a loose stop is the one combination with no defence.

4. The date is the day after the last event that could confirm the thesis, or the day the money must go elsewhere, whichever comes first. It is not chosen for comfort.

*In this book:* Chapter 2.

### 5. Loot a Burning House · 趁火打劫 (第5计)

*The looter does not light the fire. He stands at the door and takes what the fire throws out.*

1. The fire is fear of a dated event, and fear has a price. Divide the at-the-money straddle by the share price: that is the move the chain expects.

2. Set it against the last eight to twelve moves the stock made after the same event. Far above that history, with volatility high in its range, the buyer is the one being looted. Far below it, with volatility near its lows, the event is being sold cheap.

3. Between the two there is no trade. A fire measured at the market's own estimate is not a fire. It is a price.

4. The second fire is bad news that has pushed a business below what it can survive at. It is measured in the filings, not the chain.

5. Two fires, two measures. If you cannot say which one you are looting, you are standing in the third, the one you lit yourself.

*In this book:* Chapter 12.

### 6. Clamour in the East, Strike in the West · 声东击西 (第6计)

*Drums in the east, the army crosses in the west. The noise is not a lie about the war. It is a lie about the place.*

1. The edge at one level is not the edge at another. A finding about stocks is a finding about stocks until it has been tested in the option that will carry it.

2. The option adds a clock, a spread and a price of fear the stock never had. A strong stock result can become a losing options trade without any error in the stock result.

3. Ask where the edge lands before choosing the instrument. Sometimes it is the option. Sometimes it is the shares, and the option only adds a clock the thesis does not need.

4. A backtest in one instrument, presented as a strategy in another, is a feint played on yourself.

*In this book:* Chapter 5.

## Set II: Stratagems for Confronting the Enemy 《敌战计》

### 7. Create Something From Nothing · 无中生有 (第7计)

*Straw men went over the wall at night. The besiegers loosed at them until the quivers were empty, and by dawn the garrison had arrows.*

1. A backtest turned until it rises is a straw man at dusk. Nothing was found. Something was made.

2. Write before you look: the rule, the instrument, the entry, the exits, the size, and the result that would prove the rule false. Date the page. Do not edit it.

3. Count the trials. Ten filters tried and one kept is one kept out of ten, and it carries the luck of the nine discarded.

4. Pre-registration does not create an edge. It makes the absence of one visible, which is worth more than most edges.

5. He who can kill his own rule on the day the test fails has gained what the rule could never give him: a method.

*In this book:* Chapter 6; the failed pre-registered test in Chapter 15.

### 8. Secretly Cross at Chencang · 暗渡陈仓 (第8计)

*The plank road was rebuilt in plain sight while the army crossed at Chencang, on a road nobody watched.*

1. An earnings date is a plank road. The crowd repairs it for weeks and on the morning after looks only at the gap.

2. The gap is the noise. The drift that follows, as estimates and guidance are slowly revised, is the road.

3. Before the print, the option carries the event inside its price. After the print, that premium is gone, and the same directional thesis is bought cheaper.

4. A debit spread a few weeks out, the near strike bought and the far one sold, pays for the road and not again for the gate.

5. Measure the drift in the name, over its own past reports, before trusting it. A pattern that is famous is also crowded.

*In this book:* Chapter 6.

### 9. Watch the Fire From Across the River · 隔岸观火 (第9计)

*The house on the far bank is burning. The general does not cross to loot it. He lets the fire finish its argument.*

1. An event is a fire, and the straddle is the price of standing near it. When the price of standing near is greater than the heat, the far bank is the better post.

2. A priced event is a settled argument. The fear is already in the premium.

3. Watching is a position. Flat has a profit and loss of zero and keeps the quiver full.

4. "I have no view" is not the same as "there is no edge." Sometimes the edge is the refusal to pay for one.

5. The exception is an event measured as mispriced, with the number that would show it wrong written down before the event. The default is the far bank.

*In this book:* Chapter 12.

### 10. Hide a Dagger Behind a Smile · 笑里藏刀 (第10计)

*The host smiles, fills the cup, and speaks of old friendship. The blade is under the sleeve.*

1. The far weekly smiles: a few cents buy a fortune at a strike the price almost never reaches. The smile is the price. The dagger is the probability.

2. Count the smile: the spread paid in and out, and the rent charged every day the far strike stays far.

3. The far strike must be crossed twice, once by the stock and once by the clock. The first is unlikely. The second is certain.

4. An option whose delta is two in a hundred has, by the market's own rough reckoning, about one chance in fifty of finishing in the money, and needs more than that to pay.

5. The buyer who wants the tail must buy time with it, or a nearer strike, or nothing.

*In this book:* Chapter 6.

### 11. Sacrifice the Plum for the Peach · 李代桃僵 (第11计)

*A worm came to the peach root, and the plum tree beside it took the bite. The peach lived.*

1. The far strike is the plum. Sell it, and the near strike is bought cheaper; the debit that remains is the whole risk.

2. Buy the 100 call, sell the 110, pay 3: maximum loss 3, maximum gain 7, breakeven 103, and a breakeven win rate of the debit over the width, three in ten.

3. A long call at ten to one needs one win in eleven, and something near a doubling. The spread needs three in ten, and a three-point move.

4. Know which tree you are in. If the thesis is a doubling, the spread caps it. If the thesis is a modest move, the single leg is a donation.

5. For the seller the plum is the short leg cut on the rule, and the peach is the account, guarded by the wing he keeps.

*In this book:* Chapter 8.

### 12. Take the Goat in Passing · 顺手牵羊 (第12计)

*The column passed the village at dusk. A goat stood by the road, and a soldier led it away without breaking step.*

1. The seller's goat is the volatility premium. Most days it stands by his road: implied above realised, no event on the calendar.

2. The buyer who pays for calm feeds the seller. Calm is what the seller sells, and calm is usually priced.

3. The goat is the buyer's only when it is untended: after a crush, implied below realised, with a dated event still ahead.

4. Quiet is not cheap. A stock at a three-year low in realised volatility is a description, not a signal, and a mechanical test of buying that quiet failed.

5. Take it in passing, small, on the way. A goat is not hunted for a season.

*In this book:* Chapter 6; the test in Chapter 15.

## Set III: Stratagems for Attack 《攻战计》

### 13. Beat the Grass to Startle the Snake · 打草惊蛇 (第13计)

*The beater strikes the grass, not the snake, and the rustle tells him more than any map.*

1. A paper trade is grass beaten at no price. Nothing is at stake, and the ground still answers.

2. Thirty to fifty forward paper trades on one setup, each recorded before money touches it: forward, not fitted to the past.

3. The first probe with money is the smallest position the market will carry. A probe that costs a real share of the budget is a position wearing a probe's coat.

4. What the beating shows is the ground: whether the strike fills, how wide the spread sits, whether the move arrives on the day.

5. One setup, one patch of grass, until its habits are known.

*In this book:* Chapter 9.

### 14. Borrow a Corpse to Return the Soul · 借尸还魂 (第14计)

*The dead man is carried home so that the soul may enter the house it knew. The body is borrowed; only the soul must be living.*

1. An old screen is a corpse that still walks, in the direction of the last data it was fed.

2. A ratio is only as fresh as its oldest input. A short ratio built on a share count from years-old filings can rank a name as crowded when it is not.

3. Ask of every input: when was this last measured, by whom, and would a fresh count move the ranking?

4. A backtest fed on restated data is a soul returning to the wrong body, and it walks convincingly.

*In this book:* Chapter 15.

### 15. Lure the Tiger Down the Mountain · 调虎离山 (第15计)

*The tiger cannot be fought on its mountain. It is drawn down to ground chosen before the bait is set.*

1. An illiquid strike is the tiger's mountain. The buyer who climbs to it pays the mountain's price, and the mountain keeps the difference.

2. Count the ground before the thesis: open interest at the strike, volume on the day, and the width of the bid-ask against the mid.

3. A wide spread is a tax charged before the thesis begins, on the way in and again on the way out, right or wrong.

4. Limit orders at mid. Price is the only term the buyer can still choose, and the chased fill is a loss taken in advance.

5. A strike that cannot be sold has not been bought. Better a small position on open ground than a large one in a cave.

*In this book:* Chapters 15 and 16.

### 16. To Catch, First Let Go · 欲擒故纵 (第16计)

*The net is opened on one side, not from mercy, but so that the quarry runs into the side that is closed.*

1. A plan made at entry has targets in it. A target named after the price has moved is a wish with a number attached.

2. Release part at the first target: the trade can no longer cost the whole debit, and what has come back is the right to choose again.

3. Release the rest on a date, not on a feeling. The remainder is the only part that can pay for the losers.

4. Sell everything at the first target and the buyer has bought a wage. Sell nothing and he holds a lottery ticket with a thesis attached.

5. Write both lines before entry: the price at which part is released, and the date at which all of it is.

*In this book:* Chapter 7.

### 17. Toss a Brick to Attract Jade · 抛砖引玉 (第17计)

*The brick is thrown to bring back jade. It is worth little, and that is why it can be thrown.*

1. A candidate is a brick. Most are discarded, and the discarding is the work.

2. The gates stand in order: liquidity, a dated catalyst, volatility, structure, risk, edge. Each removes, and none is skipped.

3. Liquidity first, because nothing else can be priced without it. Edge last, because it is the gate that fails quietly.

4. Five to ten leave the funnel, at most. Some weeks none, and the funnel is still working. A funnel that passes fifteen has a gate left open.

*In this book:* Chapter 15.

### 18. To Catch Bandits, Catch Their King · 擒贼擒王 (第18计)

*A band of thieves is not broken by chasing every man. Take the chief, and the band loses its head and its plan.*

1. Fifteen positions are fifteen bandits: each small, each loud, none of them the king.

2. One tested setup is the king; one or two independent theses are his court.

3. Independence is not a label. Two call spreads in the same sector are one thesis wearing two tickers.

4. Test the king: the entry, the exit, the failure. The untested bandits will lose on their own.

5. Attention is the scarce capital, and it does not diversify. The account that watches fifteen things watches nothing.

*In this book:* Chapter 15.

## Set IV: Stratagems for Confused Situations 《混战计》

### 19. Remove the Firewood From Under the Pot · 釜底抽薪 (第19计)

*To still a boiling pot, no one ladles the water. A man draws the firewood from beneath it, and the boil dies of itself.*

1. A loss is the boiling; the size that feeds it is the firewood. To argue with the price after entry is to ladle.

2. Before the first order, write the worst case in one sentence: my worst case is this many dollars, because the debit is this.

3. Divide the risk budget by the worst case of one unit, and round down. If one contract is over the budget, take a narrower spread, a smaller underlying, or no trade. Never round up to one.

4. Risk one to make ten is a constraint before it is a hope. The one must be small enough to lose ten times in a row and still count the eleventh.

*In this book:* Chapter 4.

### 20. Fish in Troubled Waters · 混水摸鱼 (第20计)

*The fisherman who cannot see the bottom stirs the mud, and the fish rise gasping.*

1. In a high-volatility market the water is already muddy. Protection and lottery tickets are bought in the same hour, and both are marked up.

2. Premium is dearest when buying feels most urgent. The crowd is not paying for information; it is paying for its own alarm.

3. A long single leg in that water buys the alarm at its peak. A debit spread hands part of the inflated premium back through the strike it sells.

4. The far bank of muddy water, where income looks easiest, is the seller's ground and needs a seller's reserve. The buyer does not cross in a panic.

*In this book:* Chapter 10.

### 21. The Golden Cicada Sheds Its Shell · 金蝉脱壳 (第21计)

*The cicada leaves the husk whole and empty on the branch, and the bird strikes the husk.*

1. A position carried into expiry is that husk. The shape is still on the screen; the life has gone out of it.

2. Leave before the last days, not on them. The final week is where an illiquid short leg is pinned against a strike with nowhere to go.

3. Know what settles, and how. Index options settle in cash, at a morning print or a close. Options on a fund or a stock are claims on shares, and shares can be assigned.

4. The trader who writes the exit at entry keeps the choice of the shell. The one who waits for expiry hands that choice to the clearing house.

*In this book:* Chapter 17.

### 22. Shut the Door to Catch the Thief · 关门捉贼 (第22计)

*When the thief is in the house, shut the door. Do not chase him into the fields, where the ground knows him better than it knows you.*

1. The plan is the door, and it shuts at entry. After that the thief is inside terms already written.

2. Do not move the stop because the price came close. Do not widen the spread because the debit became uncomfortable. Do not extend the time stop because the thesis is "still intact."

3. "Give it room" is the thief's phrase, spoken in the trader's voice.

4. Tightening is permitted. Loosening is the theft. A plan that may be revised under loss is a preference with a schedule.

*In this book:* Chapter 10.

### 23. Befriend the Distant, Attack the Near · 远交近攻 (第23计)

*The far state is courted with a treaty; the near field is taken acre by acre.*

1. Five bullish call spreads in five technology names look like five distant states. They answer to one throne.

2. When one rate, one index or one fear moves all five, the five are one bet. Give that one bet one risk budget, not five.

3. Count the heat: what all open trades lose together, not what each loses alone. Keep it to a small, fixed share of the account, written in advance.

4. The distant ally is the trade that shares no catalyst with the rest: another driver, another date, another mechanism. It is a different reason, not a different ticker.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number, appears in Chapter 5, where the insurer calls it accumulation.

### 24. Borrow the Road to Conquer Guo · 假道伐虢 (第24计)

*Jin borrowed the road through Yu to reach Guo. Guo fell, and on the march home Yu fell with it.*

1. Single-stock options and index options are two wars. They share a screen and a vocabulary, not a method.

2. The single name asks: which company event, whose guidance, what skew, what borrow. The index asks: which macro date, what term structure, what premium for variance, how the contract settles.

3. An edge tested on one name does not become an index rule by being applied to more names.

4. Two calendars, two sets of filters, two tests, two journals. March each army on its own road and borrow nothing but the discipline.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number: the seller's index spreads of Parts I to IV and the buyer's single-name attacks of Part V are two such roads.

## Set V: Stratagems for Gaining Ground 《并战计》

### 25. Replace the Beams With Rotten Timbers · 偷梁换柱 (第25计)

*Swap the beams for rotten timber, paint them true, and the roof still sits, until the first snow.*

1. A hit rate is put where a win rate belongs. That a stock doubled within a quarter is a fact about the stock, not the record of a stopped option trade.

2. A hit rate counts arrivals. A trade counts exits: the premium paid, the date of the stop, the price at which the spread can be sold.

3. The second rotten beam is a rule that was never a rule. "Buy cheap convexity when the stock is quiet" sounds like a method. It is one man's judgement, compressed into a sentence by someone else.

4. When the beam is judgement, no filter cut from it will carry the same load. The test of Mai's quiet, in Chapter 15, is that beam removed.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number, appears in Chapter 15.

### 26. Point at the Mulberry, Curse the Locust · 指桑骂槐 (第26计)

*A man points at the mulberry and curses it, and the locust on the next branch mends its ways.*

1. A trade card names the evidence and names the absence of evidence; the reader is meant to hear both.

2. "Research, not yet proven, paper only" is not a warning stuck to the card. It is the card.

3. Say what was tested: which structure, which years, which costs, which exits, which sample. Say what was not.

4. A label that claims less than the evidence is honest. A label that claims more moves real risk onto a beam nobody has tested.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number, is the rule of every chapter: no forecasts, no signals, no performance claims.

### 27. Feign Madness but Keep Your Balance · 假痴不癫 (第27计)

*The general wears the fool's mask in camp and keeps his accounts by night.*

1. A day with no qualifying trade looks like a wasted day. It is a position, and often the right one.

2. To invent a candidate so that the day has a trade is to mistake activity for edge.

3. Record the empty day as a result: the candidates reviewed and why each was refused. Then it is evidence of work, not absence of it.

4. Folly that believes itself is no longer feigned. Wait; the count does not expire with the session.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number, appears in Chapter 3, the buyer's declined book.

### 28. Remove the Ladder When the Enemy Has Climbed · 上屋抽梯 (第28计)

*Invite a man onto the roof, then take the ladder away. He goes forward or he leaps.*

1. The buyer builds his ladder before he climbs: a first tranche, then rungs at prices written in advance.

2. The rungs are confirmation levels, not consolation levels. They are climbed when the thesis confirms, never because the price has fallen.

3. The whole ladder is measured against the maximum loss before the first step.

4. Once on the roof, the ladder comes away. Averaging down is not a ladder. It is a rope tied to the wrong beam.

*In this book:* Chapter 11.

### 29. Deck the Tree With False Blossoms · 树上开花 (第29计)

*Silk blossoms are tied to a bare tree. From the road it is spring; up close it is string and dye.*

1. One spread that returned several times its debit makes a season, if it is the only one shown.

2. Look at the median, not the blossom. The middle trade is what the method usually does.

3. Look at the whole distribution: the worthless expiries, the trades never filled, the candidates the rule found and could not trade.

4. Many small losses and a few large gains is a description of a distribution, not a promise about the next trade.

5. The question is never "did most win." It is whether the few paid for all the rest, after costs, in a sample large enough to trust.

*In this book:* the idea, not the number, appears in Chapter 16.

### 30. Make the Host and the Guest Exchange Roles · 反客为主 (第30计)

*The guest comes in by the side door, stays for supper, and by morning is answering the door as host.*

1. The guest is the process: the calendar, the five sentences, the size, the time stop, the journal. It must end as master of the house.

2. The journal records facts, not verdicts. "The stop was moved" is a fact. "The trader is a fool" is a verdict, and a verdict teaches nothing.

3. Count the week: the number of trades, the expectancy after costs, the deepest drawdown, the rules broken.

4. Expectancy is arithmetic: the hit rate times the average win, minus the miss rate times the average loss, minus costs.

5. When the process keeps the house, the trade is a guest. When the trade keeps the house, the process has been shown the door.

*In this book:* Chapter 18.

## Set VI: Stratagems for Desperate Situations 《败战计》

### 31. The Beauty Trap · 美人计 (第31计)

*A beauty is not found; she is prepared: the dress, the lamp, the angle of the face. So is a backtest.*

1. Test her in the room where she was found, then in a room she has never seen. Fix the rule on one period, judge it on another, and keep a third untouched to the last day.

2. The untouched period is the only honest witness. Its door opens once.

3. A rule and its data, chosen together, cannot acquit each other.

4. Prefer the curve with a scar in it. The buyer needs this more than the seller, because one beautiful run of long calls is exactly what a fitted sample produces.

*In this book:* Chapter 16.

### 32. The Empty Fort · 空城计 (第32计)

*The gates stood open, the streets were swept, and a man sat on the wall with his lute.*

1. An empty fort cannot be dressed. A pretended garrison is found out; an open gate is not.

2. In a drawdown the account has nothing to show. Show nothing: fewer trades, smaller, slower.

3. The position not taken cannot lose. Its maximum loss is known to the cent, and it needs no stop.

4. When the options fort has no strike, no bid and no open interest, the same judgement may hold another wall, the shares themselves. The cheapest expression is not always an option.

*In this book:* Chapter 16.

### 33. Let the Enemy's Spy Sow Discord · 反间计 (第33计)

*A letter left on the desk for the spy to steal has done its work before it is read.*

1. The option chain is the enemy's spy, reporting daily what others fear: the implied move, the skew, the term structure, the price of borrow.

2. An intercepted report is useful twice: for what it says, and for why someone wished it said.

3. Skew is the spy's accent; it tells you which tail he fears. The term structure is his calendar.

4. Stale data is a double agent. It answers with yesterday's map and charges today's price.

5. The deepest secret is the shape: a smooth curve laid over an event that can only go two ways. Read where the map is wrong.

*In this book:* Chapter 13.

### 34. Inflict Injury on Oneself to Win Trust · 苦肉计 (第34计)

*A general is beaten before his own camp and goes over to the enemy with the wounds still open. He is believed because the wounds are real.*

1. The stop is the buyer's wound, taken by his own hand before the enemy can take it for him.

2. Thirty to fifty trades are the only wound that teaches. Below that, the sample is an opinion.

3. Count the hit rate against the breakeven win rate, after costs. If the setup does not clear that line by a comfortable margin, it is killed.

4. The kill rule is written while the setup is still beautiful. Written afterwards, it is a eulogy. A setup you cannot kill is the one that kills you.

*In this book:* Chapter 16.

### 35. Chain Stratagems · 连环计 (第35计)

*The ships were lashed together so the fleet would ride steady in the swell. The steadiness was the gift, and one fire was the price.*

1. A trade is a chain: the calendar, the five sentences, the size, the alert, the log, the review. One weak link and the rest is cargo.

2. Sunday: the dated events on one calendar, the volatility ranks, the liquidity, five to ten candidates, no more.

3. The exit is written while the head is cool, not decided while the screen is red. After the trade, the log; each week, the review.

4. A chain is a routine that runs in the weeks you do not feel like running it. And a book chained to one factor is the lashed fleet: steady in calm water, burning all at once.

*In this book:* Chapter 5.

### 36. If All Else Fails, Retreat · 走为上 (第36计)

*Of the thirty-six, the last is to march away: not the proudest, but the one that keeps the army.*

1. When "I have to make it back" speaks, the defeat is no longer on the field. It is in the man.

2. Revenge is a position with no stop and no catalyst.

3. Retreat to paper, or to a size too small to hurt, until the trades can be read without shame.

4. The account that leaves the field returns to it. The account that stays to prove something does not.

5. The last stratagem is the first: know the way out before you take the field. What comes home is the capital, the log, and the habit of returning.

*In this book:* Chapter 16.
