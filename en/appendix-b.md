# Appendix B: The Underwriter's Ledger

*承保人的账本*

The seller's half of this book reads the credit spread as an insurance policy, and the insurer's rules are scattered through the chapters where the general's lines call for them. Here they are on one page: thirteen rules, one for each part of an insurance company, with the chapter that reads each at length. Read the page before the first order of the day, and again on the day a rule was broken.

| # | The rule | In one sentence | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The policy** | Read all five terms off the ticket, premium, attachment point, limit, term and size, and say aloud: *I am paid this to cover that, the limit is the width, the term ends on this day, and I can pay the full claim.* | 1 |
| 2 | **The price of fear** | Sell only when the premium, the market's fear, is larger than the loss cost, the market's truth, as measured in your own table and not in last month's mood. | 6 |
| 3 | **Underwriting** | Most of the work is saying no: exclude the known hazard on the calendar, distrust the sudden eager buyer, and keep a book of the policies you declined. | 3 |
| 4 | **The law of large numbers** | Many small policies, each survivable, all priced; size before frequency, and never raise the size after a good month or a bad one. | 2 |
| 5 | **Accumulation** | Count the coasts, not the houses: ask what the whole book pays if the market falls five percent before the term ends, and give that number a limit. | 5 |
| 6 | **Limits and deductibles** | Every policy carries a wing; know which deductible you wrote; and remember that a limited policy written too many times is an unlimited book. | 4 |
| 7 | **Claims** | Decide the settlement before the sale, settle small and early, hedge into the condor only when hurt, and never write the next policy to make back the last claim. | 7 |
| 8 | **Reserves** | Keep a fixed share of the account unused, measured in full claims it can pay; refill it before writing again. | 2 |
| 9 | **The combined ratio** | Claims plus costs, divided by premium, kept monthly from the journal; above one hundred, stop and find which line is wrong; drop the line that carries the losses. | 13 |
| 10 | **Catastrophe** | Run the storm on paper while the sun shines; if the paper storm closes the account, the size is wrong today. | 12 |
| 11 | **Reinsurance** | The wing is cover bought with part of the premium; buy back the short at a few cents; cede enough to sleep and keep enough to earn. | 17 |
| 12 | **Float** | The credit is premium held in trust, earned by the clock; never count it, or size on it, before the term has passed. | 9 |
| 13 | **The hundred-year company** | Write less in the soft market and carefully in the hard one; keep the rules in writing; review the journal on a calendar; change a rule only on evidence, never on the day it was broken. | 18 |

## The seller's rule line

Said once, here, because the first edition and its companions said it too often:

**Size over frequency. A limit on every position. One side first. Hedge into the condor only when hurt. Cut the short leg and keep the wing. Never stack a second position on the same direction.**

## The buyer's line, for the facing page

The buyer has no ledger of thirteen. He has five sentences, said before he pays, which are given in full in Chapter 15:

**The edge, as a price. The catalyst, with a date and a mechanism. The maximum loss, as a number. The time stop, chosen from the catalyst. The breakeven win rate, the debit over what the ticket pays when right.**

And one line that both sides share:

**When the reason is gone, the trade is gone. The money follows.**
